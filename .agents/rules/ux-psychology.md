# UX Psychology & Interaction Guidelines

## 1. Goal Gradient ("Never Start at 0%")
- In wizards, progression paths, or onboarding flows, credit initial momentum immediately.
- Example: Viewing the sequence track initializes the journey with baseline credit (e.g. *Step 1 initialized = 20% complete*).

---

## 2. Smart Defaults & Decision Friction
- Pre-select recommended views or filters to minimize decision fatigue (70–90% adherence rule).
- Never present users with empty, intimidating, or ambiguous choices.

---

## 3. Evaluative Ease & Transparency
- **Kill Ambiguous Ranges:** Display single, exact numbers. Avoid vague estimates or wide ranges.
- **Show, Don't Tell:** Ground metrics in authentic, date-stamped media proofs, verifiable data sources, and explicit schemas.
- **Sensory Copy:** Use plain-language, direct phrases rather than internal acronyms or obscure jargon.

---

## 4. Competence Over Badge Theater
- Focus on real output, verified metrics, and genuine milestones.
- Avoid hollow gamification (arbitrary points, meaningless digital badges).
- Progress rings and roadmap nodes must reflect actual verified data records.

---

## 5. Canonical UX & Component-First Mandate
- **Solve Canonically First**: Rely on canonical UX patterns and interaction primitives unless stated otherwise.
- **Find a Canonical Component First**: For feedback, dialogs, progress indicators, and status messages, always reuse canonical UI components (`Toast`, `Modal`, `Drawer`, `StatusPill`) rather than manufacturing ad-hoc interactive overlays.
- **Build Canonical When Repeatable**: When a repeatable UX pattern emerges, extract and build a canonical component first before utilizing it.
